About

A fully licensed, accredited preschool

The National Academy of Early Childhood Programs accredits West Falmouth Preschool, a fully licensed preschool in the state of Massachusetts. The state Department of Early Education and Care licenses West Falmouth Preschool.

The staff of West Falmouth Preschool consists of a lead teacher/director, a teacher and a teachers' assistant. Each teacher has their Bachelor's Degree in Education and is qualified to teach by the Department of Early Education and Care (EEC). Each teacher is certified in pediatric first aid and CPR. Together, the teachers of WFPS have many years of experience with children of this age group.

Meet our teachers

Our mission

West Falmouth Preschool is a parent cooperative that nurtures and develops a child's love of learning, independence and creativity. We provide a positive learning environment where children build self-confidence through a flexible, semi-structured curriculum of social and limited academic skills, with kindergarten readiness as our goal.

What is cooperative?

How parents cooperate in the preschool

  • One main annual fundraiser. Parents raise $200 for each child enrolled and help the fundraising chair organize the event. Other fundraising events through the year are voluntary. Fundraising is very important, since all funds raised benefit the children directly.
  • A day in the classroom. Parents assist in the classroom from 9 a.m. to 1 p.m. one day per school year, and are welcome to volunteer more. Look for sign-ups on the parent board, or talk to the director about special interests you'd like to bring to the classroom.
  • Spring and fall clean-ups. Children are always welcome to help too!
  • Lending a hand. Parents may be asked to help with minor repairs or maintenance, snow removal, enrichment activities, or to sub if a teacher is out sick.
  • Graduation. Non-graduating families host graduation for graduating families: helping set up and clean up, and sometimes providing refreshments for the celebration at the school.

Who runs the school

The Preschool consists of the teaching staff, the students and the Executive Committee. The Executive Committee and the Director make the day-to-day decisions on the operation of the Preschool. Each year the parents elect five officers (President, Vice President, Treasurer, Corresponding Secretary and Recording Secretary), who in turn appoint up to six non-voting positions (Health Agent, Registrar, Parent-Teacher Liaison, Fundraising Coordinator, Maintenance Chair, and Publicity-Social Events Coordinator). These eleven people are exempt from clean-up responsibilities.

The Executive Board meets monthly and holds three mandatory parent meetings (September, January and May). Monthly Executive meetings are open to all who want to attend.

History

Raising the roof

For many years the preschool was housed in the basement of the West Falmouth Library. Today it sits nestled back on 28 Blacksmith Shop Road, just around the corner from the library, in its very own building: a little red one-room schoolhouse.

The school is named after Director Elizabeth Ghiorse Osgood's parents, John and Ruth Ghiorse, who were lifelong educators. Mr. and Mrs. Peter Ghiorse of Stuart, FL (Mrs. Osgood's brother) made a leading contribution to secure the naming rights of the building. Mr. John Austin of West Falmouth and Mr. Steve Good also made leading donations. The playground is named after Mr. Austin, who provided the school with its flagpole, and Mr. Good donated the antique brass bell that still rings from the cupola.

2002

Director Elizabeth Ghiorse Osgood asks Steve Good of Good Builders, Inc., whose son was enrolled at the time, to look at the property. After walking the land, he believes the project can be done, and he's willing to do it.

"Raising the Roof after 50 years"

Parents and staff set out to build their own schoolhouse. The capital campaign raises roughly $200,000 in less than 18 months, through generous donations from individuals and businesses in Falmouth and surrounding communities.

The plans

Architect Christopher Doonan, an extended family member of the preschool community, draws the construction plans and, with several parents, guides them through permitting.

2004–2005

Good Builders, Inc. builds the school. Upper Cape Tech students build the cupola, and Falmouth Engineers, Inc. does all the site work. The schoolhouse opens for the 2005 school year.
